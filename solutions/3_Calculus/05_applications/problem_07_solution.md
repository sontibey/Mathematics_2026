# Exercise 7. Exponential Growth

Assume a positive initial population $N_0>0$. Differentiating gives

$$
N\prime(t)=N_0ke^{kt}=kN(t).
$$

Doubling requires $N(T)=2N_0$, hence $e^{kT}=2$ and

$$
T=\frac{\ln2}{k}.
$$

The initial population cancels, so the doubling time depends only on the relative growth constant $k$. In fact $N(t+T)/N(t)=e^{kT}=2$ for any starting time. If $N_0=0$, the population is identically zero and a positive-population doubling interpretation does not apply.
