# Exercise 7. Limit at a Point and Canceling a Factor

For $x\ne3$,

$$
\frac{x^2-4x+3}{x-3}=\frac{(x-1)(x-3)}{x-3}=x-1.
$$

Therefore the limit is $3-1=2$. A limit depends on values arbitrarily near the point, with the point itself excluded, so the original expression need not be defined at 3. Its graph is the line $y=x-1$ with a hole at $(3,2)$.
